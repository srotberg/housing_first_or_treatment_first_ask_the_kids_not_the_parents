\section{The rule}

\begin{quote}
\emph{``maybe the right way to do this is to have the parent to have a child only
when they are young and have no kids when they are old. then children are only
with the young! and the young transmit the SUD penalty only if they get it in
the transition to being old. The old dont weight the child at all since it left,
so they only care about their own consumption and not the child's.''}

\emph{``if the old die with SUD, that does not transmit to the child, since the
child is already young at that time.''}
\end{quote}

\noindent A child is in the household for exactly one period, the parent's
period~1. It leaves at the end of it, and it leaves as a young adult, which is
why the cohort arriving each period has mass one. Everything that happens to the
parent after that happens to somebody the child no longer lives with. So the
equivalence scale is $\xi=\sqrt2$ in period~1 and $1$ in period~2; the old carry
no child term; and the last moment that can transmit anything to the child is
the \emph{transition} out of period~1, where the disorder is realised.

\paragraph{The demography closes exactly.} At a date there is mass $1$ of
children, mass $1$ of young, and mass $\bar s=\phi_0(1-\kappa\bar\pi)$ of old.
This period's children are next period's young, so the cohort entering is mass
$1$ forever: no child is ever between households, and no period of a life is
spent in nobody's household. That is what fails if a child straddles the
transition --- then a parent's death truncates a childhood and the childhood
flow has to be survival-weighted.

\paragraph{The weights add up, which is why the child's weight is $\sqrt2-1$.}
A household of two counts as $\xi=\sqrt2$ adult equivalents and reaches the
utility of one person holding $1/\xi$ of its resources. Give the parent weight
$1$ and the child $\xi-1$ and the weights sum to $\xi$ exactly: with no drugs
$X_P=X_K=X$ and $u(X_P)+(\xi-1)u(X_K)=\xi\,u(c/\xi,h/\xi)$, so nothing is
created or lost by the household having a child. A weight of $1$ would count two
people in full while deflating their bundle as though there were $\sqrt2$.

\section{The two periods, written out}

\paragraph{Period 2 --- terminal, no child.} State: assets $a$, income $y$,
disorder $d'\in\{0,1\}$. Employment is drawn ($u_0$ clean, $u_1$ with the
disorder), a voucher is drawn subject to the policy rule, housing is chosen from
$\{$large rental, small rental, street$\}$. Assets are run down and nothing is
bequeathed, so $c=Ra+\text{inc}-\text{rent}$. There is no drug choice.
\[
  X_{P2}=c^{\varphi_c}h^{\varphi_h}\bar b^{\,\varphi_q},
  \qquad
  W_2(a,y,d')=\max_{h}\; u(X_{P2})+\aleph,
  \qquad u(x)=\tfrac{x^{1-\sigma}}{1-\sigma}.
\]
$\xi=1$ and the child term is absent. This is already the rule.

\paragraph{Period 1 --- the child is present.} State: income $y$, an employment
draw and a voucher draw, giving $(\text{inc},r_{\text{mul}})$. The household
chooses $v\ge0$, housing and saving $a'$, with
$c=\text{inc}-p_v v-\text{rent}-a'$ and $\xi=\sqrt2$:
\[
  X_P=\Big(\tfrac{c}{\xi}\Big)^{\varphi_c}\Big(\tfrac{h}{\xi}\Big)^{\varphi_h}(\bar b+v)^{\varphi_q},
  \qquad
  X_K=\Big(\tfrac{c}{\xi}\Big)^{\varphi_c}\Big(\tfrac{h}{\xi}\Big)^{\varphi_h}\ \bar b^{\,\varphi_q}.
\]
The child's bundle carries the Stone--Geary shifter alone: it consumes none of
the drug, so the drug reaches it only by taking consumption out of the shared
$c$. Onset at the transition is $\pi(v)=1-e^{-\lambda v}$, and survival is
$s_0=\phi_0$ clean, $s_1=\phi_0(1-\kappa)$ with the disorder, \emph{inside} the
expectation. The household maximises
\[
  V \;=\; \underbrace{u(X_P)+\omega_c(\xi-1)u(X_K)+\aleph}_{\text{flow}}
  \;+\;\beta\big[(1-\pi(v))s_0W_2(a',y,0)+\pi(v)s_1W_2(a',y,1)\big]
  \;-\;\underbrace{\omega_c\cdot\texttt{chdmg}\cdot\pi(v)}_{\text{continuation half}} .
\]

\noindent So $\omega_c$ acts in \textbf{two} places, and only one of them is
switched on. \texttt{chdmg} is the child's expected lifetime loss, it is
declared $0.0$ at \texttt{stylized.py:641} and nothing sets it. The continuation
half has never been live in any calibration; the reduced form was a placeholder
for a child value function that was never built.

\section{Full weight on the child}

\begin{quote}
\emph{``the young household should put the full weight on their child both in
contemporaneous consumption and the future value of their life because they
become adults right after.''}
\end{quote}

\noindent That is $\omega_c=1$ in the flow, and \texttt{chdmg} replaced by the
child's actual value function. The child becomes an adult exactly when the
parent becomes old, so both arrive one period later and take the same $\beta$:
\[
  \boxed{\;
  V_1(y)=u(X_P)+(\xi-1)u(X_K)+\aleph
  \;+\;\beta\,\mathbb{E}_{d'}\big[\phi(d')W_2(a',y,d')\big]
  \;+\;\beta\,\mathbb{E}_{d'}\big[V_1(y'(y,d'))\big]\;}
\]
\[
  \log y' = \rho\log y + (1-\rho)\log\bar y - \delta_d\mathbf{1}\{d'=1\},
  \qquad \rho=0.35 .
\]
The child's survival is not the parent's --- the child lives whether or not the
parent dies --- so $V_1(y')$ carries no $\phi(d')$; only $W_2$ does. And
\texttt{chdmg} falls out as what it always meant,
$V_1(y'_{\text{clean}})-V_1(y'_{\text{pen}})$.

\paragraph{Four things it forces.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
\item \textbf{It becomes a fixed point.} $V_1$ is on both sides, so period~1 is
no longer a backward solve: it needs iteration to a fixed point over the income
grid. At $\beta=0.86038$ the dynastic multiplier is $1/(1-\beta)=7.16$, so the
level of $V_1$ rises by roughly seven-fold and the CEV, which needs a level, has
to be recomputed against it. The mortality trade-off is unaffected: the dynastic
term does not contain $\phi_0$, so it drops out of the VSL derivative.
\item \textbf{$\omega_c$ is not a free dial.} It is calibrated, identified on
the child use gap, and came out at $0.01518$. Setting it to $1$ overrides a
moment the model currently matches to $+0.3$ per cent.
\item \textbf{Full internalisation removes the externality.} At
$\omega_c\approx0$ the damage to the child is a pure externality and there is a
child-based case for policy. At $\omega_c=1$ the parent already weighs the
child's loss in full when choosing $v$. That may be the benchmark you want, but
it is the mechanism, not a detail.
\item \textbf{Welfare then double-counts.} If the parent internalises the child
fully, $V_1$ already contains the child's whole lifetime, so summing $V_1$ over
cohorts counts each generation repeatedly. The code half-handles this and
handles it imperfectly: \texttt{own = tot + pen} adds the continuation penalty
back but leaves $\omega_c(\xi-1)u(X_K)$ inside $V_1$, so childhood is counted
$1+\omega_c$ times --- a $0.5$ per cent overcount today, a full double count at
$\omega_c=1$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The argument in its favour.} $\omega_c=1$ is the only value
consistent with the equivalence scale. The household's weights then sum to
$1+(\xi-1)=\xi=\sqrt2$, the same $\sqrt2$ that deflates its bundle. Any
$\omega_c<1$ means the household spends as $\sqrt2$ people and is counted as
fewer.

\section{$\beta=0.995$ annual, and what it costs before recalibration}

$0.995^{30}=0.86038$ over a thirty-year period. That is what
\texttt{stylized.py} carries as its default and what the September~13 sweep
overrode to $\beta=1.0$, at its upper bound. Setting it back is necessary for
the dynastic term --- at $\beta=1$ the sum $V_1=\text{flow}+V_1(\text{child})
+V_1(\text{grandchild})+\dots$ adds about $9.6$ per generation forever and does
not converge.

\textbf{It cannot simply be substituted in.} Holding every other parameter at
the calibrated vector and moving $\beta$ alone:

\section{Three of the four parts are already built; one is contradicted}

\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.40\textwidth}p{0.30\textwidth}p{0.24\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule

the SUD transmits on the transition out of period 1 & \texttt{transition.py:119}, \verb|use|$\times$\verb|prog| & built \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\medskip
\noindent The child's penalty has two legs and they key on different periods.
The \textbf{SUD leg} is \verb|use_prob|$\times$\verb|prog|: the chance the parent
uses in period~1 times the chance a user acquires the disorder at the
transition. That is the rule already. The \textbf{homelessness leg} is
\verb|terminal_homeless_prob|, the parent's chance of being on the street
\emph{in period~2} --- under the rule, exactly the state that cannot reach a
child who has left.

Period-2 keying was deliberate: it was put there so Treatment First's own
revocations could reach a child, since the voucher is withdrawn at the
transition and period-1 homelessness cannot register it.
\texttt{TRANSITION\_LESSONS.md} carries it as a standing instruction. Both
cannot hold, so \textbf{nothing has been changed}. Note that keying on period~1
does not switch conditionality off: it still reaches the child through the SUD
leg by moving how much people use. What is lost is the homelessness half of the
revocation.

\section{``SUD transmission is smaller'' --- where that bites}

Not in the stylized model. Drugs are chosen in period~1 only and the disorder is
realised at the transition, so there is no old-acquired SUD to remove: the
transmitted object already \emph{is} the period-1 one, and the share of children
born behind the SUD penalty stays at $3.86$ per cent.

It bites in the \emph{quantitative} model, where use is chosen at every age and
a parent can acquire the disorder long after its children have left. There the
rule genuinely cuts the transmitting population. Expect $\delta_d$ to rise when
it does: the penalty is calibrated to the child's SUD odds ratio, so a smaller
transmitting share is met by a larger fitted penalty and the product stays
pinned by the target. This has not been measured.
