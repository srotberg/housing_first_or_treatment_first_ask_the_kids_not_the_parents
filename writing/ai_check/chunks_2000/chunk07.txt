Block 1: Who uses drugs. The five parameters in this block set participation and, more importantly, who participates. Neither policy acts on them directly; they decide how much there is for a policy to act on.

θ, the weight on drugs in utility. It is the drug’s weight in the bundle, so it mostly sets how much a

household that uses spends on drugs, but a higher weight also makes using at all worth more. As it

rises, use therefore rises on both the extensive and the intensive margin, and with it the externalities and what either policy has to act on. It is calibrated internally, at 0.17, to aggregate drug spending by users over their aggregate income, 16.0 percent, from retail drug spending in Midgette et al. (2019) and, in NSDUH 2024, the incomes of adults aged 26 and over who use drugs. b, the marginal utility from the first unit of drugs. The marginal value of the first unit of drugs goes

as θ/b, so a larger b makes the first units worth less and a household with little to spend buys none. As b falls, the marginal utility from the first unit rises, and so do overall drug use and the externalities that policy can affect. It targets a participation rate of 8.90 percent, the NSDUH 2024 rate of a drug use disorder of any severity among adults aged 26 and over.26

πd, the chance that a year of use leads to a SUD. This is what makes using risky: the step from drug

use to a disorder. As it rises, the externalities rise, and the moral hazard of Housing First turns an extra voucher into more SUD, which Treatment First then aims to prevent. It targets the share of users who have a SUD, 41.3 percent among adults aged 26 and over in NSDUH 2024. κϕd, increased mortality with a SUD. κϕd sets how much a SUD raises the chance of dying, and mortal-

ity truncates a user’s value of life. Because this is an uninsurable risk, a rise in κϕd hits the households with the highest values of life hardest, which reverses the income gradient; as the stylized model of Section 3 shows, this makes it a key parameter. It is calibrated internally, at 4.00, to the income of users relative to the average, a value within the range of the mortality rate ratios of a SUD in the liter- ature: 3.91 in the Danish registers (Plana-Ripoll et al., 2019) and 6.6 among Norwegians in specialist care for a SUD (Heiberg et al., 2018). Users earn 62 percent of the average in the model against 74 percent in the data. ℵ, the flow value of being alive. ℵis the other half of the mortality mechanism: κϕd sets how much a

SUD raises the chance of dying, and ℵwhat the life lost is worth. As it rises, dying costs more and use falls. It targets a VSL of 80 times average labour income (Viscusi and Aldy, 2003).27
