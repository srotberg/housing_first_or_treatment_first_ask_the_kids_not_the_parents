§1 Model

What is written

The original model is untouched (housing_first_or_treatment_first_SR_v1.f90) and remains the homeownership version for an appendix. Everything below refers to the rebuild in code/simple/.

1.1 Environment and state

Overlapping generations, model ages 1–60 (real age 26–85), retirement at 66. Households die stochastically; assets of the deceased are redistributed as a uniform lump-sum bequest.

12,960 points per age. Two deliberate absences.

Housing entered with is not a state. Survival, employment transitions and the recovery hazard all condition on the chosen h', and renters face no adjustment cost, so the unit a household arrives in is payoff-irrelevant. Removing it left every aggregate identical and cut the solve by 6.6×.

Employment needs three states, not four. UI is drawn on entry to unemployment with probability 0.30 and lasts one year. A first-year non-recipient and a long-term unemployed household have identical income (zero) and identical continuations, so those two states merge exactly — a proof, not an approximation.

1.2 Choices

Housing h' ∈ {homeless, 3 rental sizes} × drug regime ∈ {abstain, use} × treatment ∈ {0,1} × savings a' on the full 60-point grid × hours worked, continuous on [0,1].

1.3 Preferences

u(c, h, v, n) = [ c^α · h^β · (v+b)^γ ]^(1−σ)/(1−σ)
                + ω_ℓ · (1−n)^(1−σ_ℓ)/(1−σ_ℓ)
                + ℵ − κ_ext·( H_agg + ω_ext·HD_agg )

α = (1−θ_h)(1−θ_v),   β = θ_h(1−θ_v),   γ = θ_v

The Cobb-Douglas weights are invariant to SUD status. Only the Stone-Geary shifter b varies, by SUD status and by mental illness. This is the AE's suggestion, and it is what kills the "reducing addiction mechanically raises utility from c and h" objection.

A single b cannot match both the participation moment and the expenditure-share moment: a b large enough to deter clean households pushes the unconstrained v* below zero, which drives SUD drug spending to the corner. Two values separate them — b(clean) pins participation, b(SUD) keeps the choice interior so θ_v pins the expenditure share. Poor mental health lowers b, raising the marginal utility of the first units of use: the self-medication channel.

ℵ is the flow value of being alive. It is what makes mortality risk deter drug use, and therefore carries the paper's central mechanism.

1.4 Drug use and addiction

Two regimes: abstain (v = 0) or use (v > 0, chosen continuously). A single addiction hazard — a clean household that uses becomes SUD next period with probability π_d, regardless of how much it consumes. No light/heavy threshold, no consumption-dependent hazard.

SUD households recover with a hazard depending on housing (lower when homeless), mental illness (lower with SMI), and treatment take-up, which is a choice with a resource cost. Clean households may initiate only up to real age 55.

1.5 Labour supply — continuous, from the FOC

Hours are pinned by the intratemporal first-order condition, solved the way the original code solves it: bracket on the sign of

u_c · d(resources)/d(hours)  −  ω_ℓ·(1−n)^(−σ_ℓ)

Two things the original does not do, both of which matter.

The residual is not monotone in hours. The food-stamp floor claws back at 100% and the voucher at κ_r = 0.30, so d(resources)/d(hours) jumps up where each transfer runs out and the residual can cross zero more than once. That is the welfare trap, not a numerical artefact: "work a little and keep the transfer" and "work properly" are genuinely distinct local optima, and the household has to compare them. A single bisection on [0,1] would silently pick whichever it happened to bracket. The interval is split at the kinks and each piece solved exactly. Voucher eligibility is a step in the continuation value rather than the budget, so the piece optimum is clamped to each side of it and both are evaluated.

The FOC is solved multiplied through by (1−n)^σ_ℓ / u_c. Both factors are positive, so no bracketing decision changes, but it removes the poles false position crawls on — u_c blowing up as resources run out, and the marginal utility of leisure blowing up as hours approach one. With Anderson–Björck rather than Illinois for the remaining asymmetry at zero hours, a root takes 6.8 evaluations instead of 22.

check_hours.f90 validates the solver against a 4,001-point brute-force scan of the identical objective.

1.6 Housing, vouchers, and the two policies

Rent is a fixed price — no housing-market general equilibrium. Homelessness is the lowest point of the housing grid, with a calibrated service flow. Voucher rules are institutional, not free: eligibility below 50% of median income, 30% tenant contribution, and a lottery probability calibrated to HUD take-up among the eligible. Vouchers pay only while renting, never while homeless.

Both detection parameters are policy-experiment objects reported as sensitivity, not calibrated: the point is precisely that perfect enforcement is not credible.

1.7 Serious mental illness

Exogenous persistent binary state. It is SMI, not "any mental illness": AMI covers ~23% of US adults including mild anxiety, while SMI is ~5.5% and is defined by substantial functional impairment. Putting a quarter of the population in a state that halves productivity would be indefensible.

This follows İmrohoroğlu and Zhao (2022) structurally, not just numerically: in their model labour-market shocks generate short homeless spells while health shocks generate chronic homelessness, and expanded disability income is the effective policy for the chronic group. Homelessness driven purely by income risk is transitory; the persistent tail needs a persistent state.

SMI affects six things: productivity conditional on working, job finding and separation, mortality, the SUD recovery hazard, b (self-medication), and eligibility for disability income when not employed. Onset is derived from the target prevalence and the recovery rate, so calibrating persistence never disturbs prevalence.

ψ₁ is normalised so p_v = 1 at the benchmark. This answers R3's worry that endogenising p_v "requires suppressing this normalization": it does not, because the normalisation moves to ψ₁. Only counterfactuals move the price.

The externality enters period utility, taken as given by households — that is what makes it a spillover rather than a private cost — but the aggregates are equilibrium objects, so policy moves them and the movement feeds back into the value of life and hence into drug decisions. This is the property κ_cons lacked.

On β and wealth / GDP = 3

GDP is measured as all factor income — labour earnings plus the return on the capital stock — with transfers (social security, UI, disability, food stamps, vouchers) excluded, because transfers are redistribution, not output. Three is the standard US private-capital-to-output ratio and is what a life-cycle discount factor is normally pinned by.

It is the right target here rather than the SCF's ~7.3 net-worth-to-household-income ratio, whose numerator is mostly housing equity a model without homeownership cannot hold and whose denominator is a household income concept, not output.

Note the arithmetic this implies: with A = 3·GDP and GDP = labour income + rA, capital income is 9% of GDP. That is low relative to a one-third capital share, and it follows from holding r fixed at 3% in a model with no production function. State it in the paper rather than leave it for a referee to find.

Method. Coordinate descent. Participation is a near-threshold function of b — below a cutoff everyone uses, above it nobody does, and in the dead zone every drug moment reads zero — so the level is pinned by bisection on log b before composition is touched, in partial equilibrium.

Status

The first-stage calibration has not converged. Every number the model currently prints is a placeholder. This is the single largest piece of work outstanding.

2.2 Second stage — short-run experimental moments

The first stage matches a cross-section. It says nothing about the responses the paper's conclusion depends on — and that is exactly what R2, R3 and the AE all identified as the crux. The second stage targets those responses directly.

These are short-run objects, computed by holding the stationary distribution fixed at the benchmark, applying the experimental treatment, and simulating forward the same number of periods as the study — not by comparing two stationary equilibria. That is a different computation from stage one and needs the transition machinery.

Why the conditionality effect is a validation check and not a target. Milby's abstract reports that the abstinence-contingent arm beat the unconditional arm, but that holds only after controlling for treatment attendance. In the raw randomised comparison, NACH vs ACH gives P = .091, and the GEE models state the two housed groups "did not differ from one another." Calibrating to a point estimate the source paper reports as insignificant would be exactly the sin R1 attacked κ_cons for.

Why the extensive/intensive distinction is the sharper test. Milby's counterfactual is literally no housing, and the effect is large. The HF RCTs compare HF to "usual services", which already include shelter or transitional housing, and the effect is small or null. So the elasticity of drug use with respect to housing access is large at the extensive margin and near zero at the intensive margin. A model reproducing both is far better identified than one matching either number alone. This is the moment R2 called the crux.

Status

The second stage is specified but not built. It needs the transition-path solver.

Two of these deserve emphasis, because R1 used them to distrust the whole quantification. The paper reported that drug users earn 41% of average income against "roughly 74%" in the data — but the 74% is computed over people with a use disorder, not people who use. For ages 26+, NSDUH gives 0.951 for users and 0.687 for moderate/severe UD. 5.4 Genuinely open modelling questions

These need a decision, not compute

R2 C2 — externality calibration

Implemented, but κ_ext = 0 in the baseline. There is no credible point estimate: hedonic shelter-siting studies measure a local, avoidable disamenity, and this model has no geography. Corbae et al.'s externality is about neighbourhood housing quality, not homelessness, so only their identification logic transfers, not the functional form.

Recommendation: keep zero as the conservative baseline, report a bounded sensitivity, and use the qualitative finding that PSH raises property values while emergency shelter lowers them — i.e. the externality plausibly differs in sign between HF and TF. That is a substantive point in HF's favour, and it is more honest than inventing a number.

R2 C3 / AE A6 — parental anxiety and intergenerational transmission

R2 lists it as a margin the paper misses but writes that it "could be discussed", and predicts it "would only amplify the welfare gains." The AE's A6 is a separate and simpler complaint: §4.1.3 uses intergenerational language without any intergenerational linkage.

Decision needed. Either delete the decorative language (cheap, fully answers A6) or add a warm-glow term at one parental age using the ρ_x = 0.5 process already specified (cheap, answers both). Recommendation: the warm-glow version — it costs one term and converts a criticism into a feature.

R1 smaller comment 1 — why a maximum experimentation age?

Still present (real age 55) and still unjustified. R1 caught a real internal tension with the U-shaped age profile of drug use in Table 4(a). Either justify it from NSDUH initiation data or drop it.

AE A16 — homelessness definition

The SUD target has been corrected, but the paper must still state explicitly that it targets literal homelessness and excludes doubling-up, and flag the sensitivity.

R2 D1 — how were the rental sizes chosen?

The grid is now 4 points instead of 7, and h_homeless is calibrated, but the three rental sizes are still set rather than calibrated. Either calibrate them or say plainly how they were chosen.

§6 Compute

Where the solve stands

4-core container, gfortran 13.3, -O3 -march=native -funroll-loops -flto.

OperationWall time
One household solve, 4-point hours grid6.2 s
One household solve, continuous hours from the FOC19.2 s

Continuous hours cost 3.1×. That is the price of exactness on the labour-supply margin and of handling the welfare-trap non-convexity correctly, and it is bounded: the FOC solve is now 6.8 residual evaluations per root, down from 22 before the rescaling.

Implication for the plan. A stationary equilibrium is roughly 25–40 household solves, so 8–13 minutes. A full coordinate-descent calibration is an overnight job here and under an hour on 40 cores. Counterfactuals, once calibrated, are minutes. The transition solver will be the expensive object, and it is the thing to build next.

§7 Order

Recommended sequence

  1. Converge the first-stage calibration. Everything in §5.1 unblocks at once, including the model-fit table whose absence is what made R1 distrust the quantification.
  2. Build the transition-path solver. The critical path: it unblocks R1 3(d) and the whole of §2.2, which is what R2, R3 and the AE independently identified as the crux.
  3. Run the second-stage calibration against Milby and the HF RCT literature, reporting the extensive/intensive contrast.
  4. Run the shutdown ladder and the sensitivity and identification figures (§5.3) — cheap once 1–3 are done.
  5. Decide the two open modelling questions (§5.4): the parental warm-glow term, and the maximum experimentation age.
  6. Rewrite, leading with the generosity result and reporting welfare and mortality as two dimensions, with the Costantini evidence confronted in the introduction.
